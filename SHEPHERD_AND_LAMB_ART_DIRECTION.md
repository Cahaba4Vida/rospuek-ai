# Jesus and the Lamb — Locked Visual Reference & Sequence Direction

Reviewed with original user-uploaded references, 2026-11-23.

## Purpose
Create a short **sequence of distinct standalone paintings** showing the lamb beginning still, prancing around Jesus, making a small joyful leap, and finally being received and embraced. The paintings must belong to **the exact same original scene**, not simply resemble it in subject matter. The initial uploaded original Jesus-and-lamb artwork is the PRIMARY MASTER for this scene. Do not regenerate/reimagine the full scene to make each moment.

## Reference priority
1. **Primary continuity master:** the user-uploaded original painting of Jesus standing on the right in shallow reflective water, looking down at a little white lamb to his left, cream draped robes and dusty-pink sash, pastel sunrise, distant soft mountains, blossoms, water reflections. Keep same painting and its handcrafted restrained texture. Its figures' sizes, faces, clothing, perspective, and landscape are fixed continuity anchors.
2. **Secondary artistic references (not layouts to mix):** (a) Christ's compassionate embrace in blue-gray fog and shallow water; (b) minimalist off-white watercolor with Christ and lamb small within enormous negative space; (c) impressionistic pink floral fields, tiny figures, unfinished dot-like flowers; (d) solemn realist chiaroscuro portrait of Christ and closely observed wool; (e) solitary dark forest and lake stillness; (f) raw impressionistic Jesus entering Jerusalem on a donkey. These clarify artistic sensibility across projects; do not insert their scenery into the original scene.
3. **General journal art direction:** the mystery, restraint, hand-painted material, and emotional truth of the established atmospheric oil painting style. The lamb sequence is its delicate, warmer variant.

## Non-negotiable visual qualities
- Handmade, fine-art **oil painting**: tactile natural brush and palette knife marks visible but not exaggerated into crunchy digital flakes; broken contours, selective detail, atmospheric blending, nuanced paint layers.
- Soft natural sunrise: peach, dusty pink, pale ivory, muted lavender and light blue-gray, natural warm peach-gold reflections. Subtle glow, not overexposed gold, neon light, glitter or HDR.
- Emotion through **gentle body language and silence**, not theatrical grins or exaggerated wide-open gestures.
- Small, believable lamb with plausible sheep anatomy, four consistent legs and hooves, normal short sheep tail, organic motions rather than dog-like play or airborne cartoon poses.
- Human face and figure consistent: same Jesus hair, beard, downward tender gaze, clothing folds, pink sash, body proportions, hands and robe hem, scene scale and camera framing.
- **Same background**: retain exact mountain ridge, pastel clouds, low sun position, pink flower banks, water's reflective horizon, focal depth, and original angle as far as editing technology permits.
- No text, religious poster decorations, extra figures, more animals, elaborate halos, cartoon expressions, fake 3D render polish.

## Storyboard: smallest necessary differences
0. **Source image (unaltered):** Lamb stands looking up at Christ, his hand extended toward it, other hand at sash.
1. **Playful first step:** Keep Jesus and backdrop *unchanged*. Lamb pivots slightly on the left, lifting a single forehoof and rippling the water.
2. **Gentle prance:** Lamb makes a modest, anatomically natural hop/circle in shallow water, still within the original lamb zone; small splashes. Jesus minimally turns his head or shifts one hand.
3. **Approach:** Lamb moves a little nearer; its forehooves lift naturally. Jesus shifts BOTH hands only as needed to receive it, without changing his facial identity, robe, or feet.
4. **Moment of catch:** Lamb rises just high enough for Christ to gather it securely near his chest; adjust Jesus arms and local drapery; keep camera and environment static.
5. **Tender embrace:** Christ securely holds the small lamb, cheek close; little sheep calm and content. Preserve original visual focus, painterly composition, emotional restraint, and scenery.

## Production and QC method
- Treat **the original uploaded painting as an immutable base** and use localized image editing / masked inpainting per frame. Edit only sheep, necessarily changed hands/arms/cloth, their immediate occlusions, and local ripples/reflections.
- Render each frame from the original master plus precise frame instructions, **not from the prior generated frame**, to avoid cumulative drift.
- Keep same portrait aspect ratio, original crop, original soft organic paint texture, figure scale and color palette.
- After each attempt, compare full original vs edit side-by-side. Reject if landscape, face, robe, sash, flower arrangement, water level, dominant colors or relative proportions visibly drift; reject extra limbs, awkward support, unnatural leap, smile-as-cartoon, or sparkle overload.
- User reviews **one test frame at a time** before creating subsequent frames. For the next test, do only Frame 1 (small prance), *not* a whole batch.

## Why previous batches failed
Repeated full-scene recreation amplified brightness, sharpened impasto into a uniform glittering/crunchy texture, changed Jesus' facial structure and expression and the robe drape, made the lamb dog-like and overanimated, changed size and positions, and shifted the painting's focus from quiet affection to theatrical jumping. These are disallowed even if each result is attractive in isolation.
